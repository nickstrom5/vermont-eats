Built around how people here eat

Star ratings won't tell you which diner car is from 1948 or which creemee stand is open in May. Green Mountain Eats is organized the way people here eat: diners and fry stands, maple creemees, cider mills, farm pizza, sugarhouses, brewery kitchens and historic inns, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source or an official tourism listing. Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out in the hills.

What stays on your phone

Green Mountain Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Green Mountain Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The diner, creemee, cider mill, farm pizza, sugarhouse, brewery kitchen, historic inn and oldest-places lists are our own research from fall 2026, with a dated 2025 or 2026 source or the Vermont Department of Tourism's own listing behind each place. The wider directory of 1,576 restaurants starts from open map listings (Overture Maps), filtered to the kinds that proved reliable where states publish restaurant lists.

Where can I get a maple creemee?

Our creemee list has 7 hand-checked places, from Morse Farm in Montpelier and Canteen Creemee Company in Waitsfield to LegenDairy in Williamstown. Most are seasonal, so check before you drive. In the web search, the list puts the nearest first once you share your location.

How do I check a Vermont restaurant's inspection?

The Vermont Department of Health licenses and inspects the restaurants that serve the public, and publishes its reports only in its own search, from May 2016 on. So the app shows no inspection results: each place links to that search, and we compute no grade.

Does it show which places serve alcohol?

No. Vermont's Department of Liquor and Lottery licenses the bars and restaurants that pour alcohol, but publishes no list we may read, so the app doesn't say either way.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
